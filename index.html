GapCareGA a plain-language guide to health coverage and care access in Georgia
For uninsured adults in Georgia's coverage gap

Health coverage is confusing on purpose. This isn't.

Georgia never fully expanded Medicaid. Roughly 180,000–200,000 adults (U.S. citizens and lawfully present residents, per the Georgia Budget & Policy Institute) earn too much for traditional Medicaid but too little for full Marketplace savings — stuck in what's called the coverage gap. GapCareGA helps you see where you stand, find care you can actually afford, and check in on how you're doing while you sort it out.

PHQ-9 — a standard screening tool

How have you been feeling?

Over the last two weeks, how often have you been bothered by each of the following? This stays on your device only — nothing is sent anywhere unless you use the chat tab.

This is a screening tool, not a diagnosis. A high score, or any trouble on the last question, is a good reason to talk to a real person — not something to sit with alone.

Your check-ins over time

Knowledge and confidence for the exam room

Advocate for yourself

Research shows women, people of color, and people with a mental health history are more likely to have their symptoms minimized or reattributed rather than investigated — a pattern researchers call diagnostic overshadowing. Knowing the terms and having language ready can make it harder to brush past you.

The chat is Claude, Anthropic's AI, and it opens in a new tab on claude.ai. It can explain Georgia coverage options and how to use this app. It does not diagnose, and it is not a substitute for Georgia Gateway, a clinic, or 211. Claude may ask you to sign in.
